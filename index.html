NASCAR senior vice president, retiredKen Clapp

I have spent sixty years putting on stock car races in the western United States. I started on a pit crew in 1955, ran NASCAR's western operations, took the sport to Japan and to Mexico, and I chair the West Coast Stock Car and Motorsports Hall of Fame.

The start

It began in a garage in Walnut Creek

I was about twelve when I started hanging around a service station garage in Walnut Creek where the local racers kept their cars.

By 1955 I was on a pit crew, and that was the year our team won the Pacific Coast Grand National title. I served in the Navy after that, at Naval Air Station Point Mugu and aboard the USS Duncan.

What came next all pointed the same way. I sold cars. I promoted races at Vallejo Speedway through the 1960s. I worked on Sears Point Raceway, the track that is Sonoma Raceway today. I spent a spell at Autoweek. None of it looked like a career at the time, and all of it turned out to be one.

The work

Putting races where there had not been any

NASCAR brought me in to run marketing for the western United States in 1977. Everything after that followed from it.

01

Western operations

NASCAR named me vice president of western operations on 16 December 1983. The job was the sport west of the Rockies: the tracks, the schedules and the people who ran them.

02

Sonoma and Phoenix

When Riverside's Cup dates had to move, I arranged for them to go to Sonoma and to Phoenix. Both are still on the schedule.

03

Japan

I led the negotiations that took NASCAR to Japan, first to Suzuka and then to Twin Ring Motegi.

04

Mexico

In 2005 I helped bring the series to the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez in Mexico City. It was NASCAR's first event in Mexico.
